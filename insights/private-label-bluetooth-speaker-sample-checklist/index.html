Checklist

9 sample checks before private-label speaker quotation

The goal is to remove uncertainty before the buyer approves branding, packaging and commercial follow-up.

1. Bluetooth pairing flow

Check first pairing, reconnect behavior, phone compatibility and whether the connection language in the manual matches the actual sample.

2. TWS pairing behavior

If TWS pairing is part of the buyer brief, test how two speakers pair, how they reconnect and what instructions the buyer must include.

3. USB and TF playback

Test local playback with realistic files, then confirm controls, supported use scenario and packaging wording before making claims.

4. AUX and charging ports

Review port labels, cover design, charging port, cable fit and whether the port panel is easy for the end user to understand.

5. Battery information

Record the listed battery profile and confirm final labeling, charging details and any usage claim for the selected version.

6. Speaker structure

Sample notes should confirm the visible speaker layout, passive radiator direction and the product details the buyer wants to publish.

7. RGB and visual identity

Review lighting effect, cabinet transparency, color direction and whether the visual style fits the target retail or online channel.

8. Packaging and accessories

Confirm box artwork, model text, manual language, cable set, inner tray and any buyer-specific packaging requirements.

9. Sample record and RFQ

Connect model name, selected version, supplied images, specification reference, buyer notes and RFQ details before quotation.

FAQ

Bluetooth speaker sample questions

What should private-label buyers check on a Bluetooth speaker sample?

Buyers should check Bluetooth pairing, TWS behavior, input modes, USB/TF playback, charging details, battery information, sound structure, RGB lighting, button layout, accessory set, packaging direction and document questions for the selected sample version.

Why should Bluetooth speaker buyers test USB and TF playback?

USB and TF playback can be important for retail channels that want local playback support. Buyers should test actual file playback behavior, controls and manual wording before making market-facing claims.

When should a buyer discuss logo and packaging for a Bluetooth speaker?

Discuss logo and packaging after the base model and sample version are clear. The logo area, box artwork, manual language, accessory list and document scope depend on the selected product.

What information belongs in a Bluetooth speaker RFQ?

Include destination market, sales channel, model interest, quantity range, sample timing, color direction, branding needs, packaging questions, accessory requirements and product documents to check.
